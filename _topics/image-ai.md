  - type: lesson
    title: What image AI is for
    body: |
      Everything so far has been about text, because that is where most workplace value lives right now. But there is a second kind of tool worth knowing: image generation.

      **Image AI** creates original pictures from a written description. Marketing teams mock up a campaign in minutes. Trainers make simple diagrams without hiring a designer. Warehouses draft safety signs. It is not perfect, and I will be honest about that, but it is real and already showing up in workplaces.

      This is a short introduction. You will learn how it works, where it fits, which tools to reach for, and how to write an image prompt that gives you something you can use.
  - type: lesson
    title: How it works, in one minute
    body: |
      You do not need the math. Just the basic idea: these tools learned from millions of images paired with descriptions, and they build a new picture that matches your words.

      That is why they can produce something beautiful in seconds, and also why they sometimes give a person six fingers. The takeaway for work: **image AI is a drafting tool, not a finished-product tool.** It gets you to a strong starting point fast. A human still needs to check it before it goes anywhere important.
- title: The tools and their limits
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Which one to reach for
    body: |
      You do not need to master every tool. You need to know which fits your situation.

      | Tool | Good for |
      |---|---|
      | **Gemini** | Quick images right inside a chat you are already using |
      | **Canva AI** | The easiest on-ramp, with templates for flyers and posts |
      | **Adobe Firefly** | Trained on licensed images, a safer pick for commercial work |
  - type: lesson
    title: Four limits that matter at work
    body: |
      These are not small bugs waiting for a patch. They come from how the tools are built.

      - **Accuracy.** Hands, text inside images, and fine details often come out wrong.
      - **Bias.** The images can lean on stereotypes from their training data.
      - **Legal uncertainty.** Who owns an AI image, and whether it copied something, is still unsettled. Be careful using them commercially.
      - **No real-world specifics.** It cannot show *your* store, *your* equipment, or *your* team. It invents a generic version.
- title: Writing a good image prompt
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: The five-part formula
    body: |
      The same idea that makes text prompts better, being specific, makes image prompts better too. For pictures, "format" includes the visual style. Try this order:

      **Subject + Setting + Style + Mood + Format**

      | Vague prompt | Specific prompt |
      |---|---|
      | "A picture of an office" | "A bright, modern dental waiting room, warm natural light, clean flat-illustration style, calm and welcoming, wide horizontal banner" |

      Vague prompts give generic, often unusable results. Specific prompts get you close to what you actually need.
  - type: activity
    title: Generate one image for your field
    body: |
      Make one image that would save you time in your real work. A flyer background, a simple diagram, a signage mock-up, your choice.

      Then be honest with yourself:

      1. Would I actually use this at work as is?
      2. Is anything in it inaccurate for my field?
      3. What one change to the prompt would make it better?

      That honest review is the habit that separates an AI user from someone who just trusts whatever pops out.
