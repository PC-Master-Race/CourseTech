- title: 'The other side: AI music and audio generation'
  intro: 'Transcription is one half of audio AI. The other half is creation: generating original music from a text prompt, with no instruments, no studio, and no royalty fees.'
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Why this matters for your business
    body: |
      If you have ever posted a reel, a property walkthrough, a classroom tutorial, or a promo clip and added music from Spotify or YouTube, you may have been flagged, muted, or had the video taken down. That is copyright law working exactly as intended.

      AI-generated music is different. Because the AI made it from scratch, there is no original artist to claim ownership, so you can use it in your videos, posts, and client presentations without worrying about a takedown, as long as you follow the site's licensing rules (which usually means a subscription).

      | Tool | What it does | Best for |
      |---|---|---|
      | **Suno** (suno.com) | Describe the mood, genre, and style in a text prompt, and it generates a full song with vocals, melody, and instruments in under 30 seconds. | Background tracks for reels, promo videos, and presentations, anything needing a full produced sound. |
      | **Udio** (udio.com) | Similar to Suno but known for more polished, studio-quality output. Describe the vibe in plain English and get a complete original track. | Higher-quality audio for professional-facing content like listing videos and business pitches. |
      | **Sonauto** (sonauto.ai) | Focused on commercial use. Generates royalty-free music for business content, with controls for tempo, mood, and length. | Owners and creators who want a consistent, brandable sound without hiring a composer. |
  - type: lesson
    title: What a music prompt looks like
    body: |
      You do not need to know anything about music. Just describe what you want in plain English:

      - **For a real estate video:** "Calm, upbeat instrumental, light piano and acoustic guitar, warm and welcoming, 60 seconds."
      - **For a florist promo:** "Cheerful spring-feeling background track, no vocals, light and airy, suitable for a flower shop social post."
      - **For a business pitch:** "Professional, confident background music, subtle electronic rhythm, modern and clean, for a 2-minute presentation."

      That is the whole prompt. The AI handles the genre, tempo, key, and instruments. You get a downloadable audio file in seconds.

      A few field examples of where this helps: a real estate agent generating a calm track for a walkthrough with no copyright risk, a florist making a seasonal promo reel with a custom soundtrack, an educator adding music to a recorded lesson without getting flagged on YouTube, and an entrepreneur building a consistent audio identity that plays in every video.

    title: It passes all four questions

      | Filter question | Answer for audio tasks | Why |
      |---|---|---|
      | **Is it repetitive?** | Yes | Every client call, meeting, and session note follows the same basic pattern, again and again. |

  - type: lesson
    title: 'One caution: consent is not optional'
    body: |
      This is the rule you cannot skip. Before you record anyone with an AI tool, a client, a student, a patient, or a colleague, you must tell them and get their agreement. This is not just good manners. In many fields it is the law.

      - **Healthcare and behavioral health:** HIPAA applies. Recording without consent in a clinical setting can create serious legal exposure.
      - **Education:** FERPA protects student information. Any AI tool handling student data needs an institutional review first.
      - **Real estate and business:** some states, like California, require every person on a call to agree to being recorded.

      The rule is simple. Say: "I am going to use an AI tool to take notes on our conversation. Is that okay?" If they say yes, go ahead. If they say no, take notes the old-fashioned way.
- title: Try it
  blocks:
  - type: activity
    title: Turn a voice note into a written note
    body: |
      Let's make audio AI real with something small.

      1. Think of a short work update, a summary of a call, a to-do list, or an idea you have been meaning to write down.
      2. Open the ChatGPT app and tap the headphone icon to start voice mode (or open Otter.ai and press record).
      3. Talk through it naturally for 30 to 60 seconds, the way you would explain it to a coworker.
      4. Read what it captured. Ask it to "turn this into a short, organized note with a few bullet points."

      Then reflect: how close was it to what you meant? Would this save you time on a task you do often? And if you record other people for this in real life, how will you ask for their okay first?
