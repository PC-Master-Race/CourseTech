- title: 'Section 1: your iPhone''s physical controls'
  intro: Every device has buttons, and your phone is no different. Knowing them means you can answer calls, take photos, and control your phone without hunting around.
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: The buttons you can feel
    body: |
      Your iPhone has real buttons you can feel and press. Here is what they are and where to find them.

      | Button and where it is | What it does |
      |---|---|
      | **Side Button** (right edge, tall narrow button your right thumb can reach) | Locks the screen (press once). Wakes the screen (press once). Powers off (hold Side and Volume Up together). Takes a screenshot (press Side and Volume Up quickly at the same time). |
      | **Volume Up** (left side, the top of the two buttons) | Makes all sounds louder: music, videos, calls, ringtone. |
      | **Volume Down** (left side, the bottom of the two buttons) | Makes all sounds quieter. |
      | **Action Button** (left side, above the volume buttons, on iPhone 15 Pro, 16, and 17) | Silences the phone by default. Can be set to open the Camera, Flashlight, or other shortcuts. |
      | **Camera Control** (right side, below the Side Button, iPhone 16 and 17 only) | Opens the Camera quickly. Slide your finger along it to zoom. Press to take a photo. |

      > Not all iPhones are the same. If you have an iPhone 14 or earlier, you may have a small Ring/Silent switch instead of an Action Button. If it shows orange, your phone is on silent, so it will not ring.
  - type: lesson
    title: The Side Button and the volume buttons
    body: |
      **The Side Button** is on the right edge. You can feel it with your right thumb. Here is everything it does:

      - Press it **once** to wake the screen up.
      - Press it **once** while the screen is on to lock the phone (it goes dark to save battery).
      - Press and **hold** for 2 seconds to bring up the menu to power off or call 911.
      - Press **Side Button and Volume Up** at the same time to take a screenshot, a picture of your screen.

      > A screenshot saves a photo of exactly what is on your screen. Great for saving directions, recipes, or important information.

      **The Volume Buttons** are on the left side. The top button turns the sound up, the bottom turns it down. When the phone is ringing, pressing either one quietly mutes the ring without sending the call to voicemail.
  - type: lesson
    title: Turning the phone off, on, and unfreezing it
    body: |
      **To turn the phone all the way off:**

      1. Press and hold the **Side Button** (right) and **Volume Up** (left, top) at the same time.
      2. Hold both for about 2 seconds.
      3. A screen appears with a white bar that says "slide to power off."
      4. Put your finger on that bar and slide it from left to right. The screen goes black.

      > If that menu appears by accident, do not worry. Tap "Cancel," or just wait 5 seconds and it disappears on its own.

      **To turn it back on:** find the Side Button on the right edge, press and hold for about 2 seconds until a silver Apple logo appears in the center, then wait about 30 seconds while it starts up.

      **To restart a frozen phone** (it stops responding when you tap), do this quickly, like a 1-2-hold rhythm. Nothing gets erased:

      1. Quickly press and release **Volume Up**.
      2. Quickly press and release **Volume Down**.
      3. Press and **hold the Side Button**, and keep holding until the Apple logo appears. Then let go.
  - type: activity
    title: 'Let''s try it together: lock, wake, and volume'
    body: |
      Goal: practice the Side Button and find your volume buttons. About 3 minutes.

      1. Hold your iPhone comfortably. Run your right thumb along the right edge until you feel a raised button. That is the Side Button.
      2. Press it **once**. Your screen goes dark.
      3. Press it **once** again. Your screen lights back up. Do this two more times to get comfortable.
      4. Now find the two buttons on the left side. Press the top one (Volume Up) three times. Press the bottom one (Volume Down) three times.

      Success: you can lock and wake your screen, and you have found your volume buttons.
- title: 'Section 2: unlocking your iPhone'
  intro: Your phone holds your photos, your messages from family, and your important information. Unlocking it is the front door. Once you are comfortable with this, you will feel much more in control.
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Three ways to unlock
    body: |
      Your iPhone has three ways to unlock. Most people use one of the first two every day, and you probably already have one set up.

      | Method | How it works |
      |---|---|
      | **Face ID** (newer iPhones, no round Home button) | The front camera looks at your face. Just look at the screen and it recognizes you. |
      | **Touch ID** (older iPhones, has a round Home button) | Rest your finger on the round Home button. Your fingerprint unlocks the phone. |
      | **Passcode** (all iPhones, always works as a backup) | Type your 4 or 6 digit number code, like a PIN at the ATM. |
  - type: lesson
    title: Face ID, Touch ID, and your passcode
    body: |
      **Face ID** is on iPhones made after 2017 with no round button at the bottom.

      1. Press the Side Button once to wake the screen.
      2. Hold the phone so you can look at it comfortably. The small camera at the top scans your face.
      3. In about a second, a small lock icon at the top opens up.
      4. Swipe up from the very bottom edge with one finger. Your Home Screen appears.

      > Face ID works even with glasses on. If it does not work the first time, just look again, no need to press anything.

      **Touch ID** is on older iPhones with a round Home button. Wake the screen, then rest your finger or thumb lightly on the round Home button for about a second. Use the same finger you registered when you set up the phone.

      **Your passcode** always works as a backup. Wake the screen, swipe up (or tap the screen on older iPhones) to bring up the number pad, and tap in your code.

      > Forgot your passcode? Do not keep guessing. After 10 wrong tries the iPhone locks you out completely. If that happens, you can reset it through Apple's account recovery, so it is not the end of the world.
  - type: activity
    title: 'Let''s try it together: unlock three times'
    body: |
      Goal: practice unlocking with your method. About 4 minutes.

      First, figure out which method you have. Look at the bottom front of your phone. A round button means Touch ID. No round button means Face ID.

      Now practice: press the Side Button to turn off the screen, wait 3 seconds, and unlock. Repeat two more times. Notice how quick it gets.

      Success: your Home Screen appears each time, and the unlock feels natural.
- title: 'Section 3: your home screen tour'
  intro: The Home Screen is the starting point for everything. It is like the living room of your iPhone. Once you know what lives here and how to move around, you will feel right at home.
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Icons, the Dock, and pages
    body: |
      After you unlock, the first screen you see is the **Home Screen**.

      **App icons** are the colorful little pictures. Each one represents a program, and tapping it once opens that program. A few common ones:

      - A green telephone handset is the **Phone** app.
      - A green speech bubble is the **Messages** app.
      - A blue compass is **Safari**, your internet browser.
      - A rainbow circle is the **Camera**.
      - A gray square with gears is **Settings**.
      - A white envelope on blue is **Mail**.

      > You do not need to memorize the icons right now. Just know that each picture opens something when you tap it.

      **The Dock** is the row of 4 apps at the very bottom. It stays put no matter which page you are on. Most phones start with Phone, Messages, Safari, and Music there, and you can change them (Section 6).

      **Pages of apps:** just above the Dock is a row of tiny dots. Each dot is one page, and the bright dot is the page you are on. Slide your finger left to see the next page, right to go back.
  - type: lesson
    title: Folders, widgets, and the App Library
    body: |
      **Folders** are groups of apps in one icon, like a "Utilities" folder holding Calculator, Compass, and Clock. Tap a folder to open it, tap an app inside to open it, or tap the gray background to close it.

      **Widgets** are larger tiles that show live information without opening an app: a weather widget shows the temperature, a calendar widget shows your next appointment, a photos widget rotates through pictures. We add one in Section 5.

      **The App Library** is one more screen to the left, past all your pages. It automatically sorts every app into groups like Social, Entertainment, and Utilities, like a filing cabinet. You can search for any app using the bar at the top.

      > Can't find an app? Swipe all the way left to the App Library and use the search bar at the top.

      **To get back to the Home Screen** from anywhere: on newer iPhones, swipe up from the very bottom edge. On older iPhones, press the round Home button.
  - type: activity
    title: 'Let''s try it together: explore and come home'
    body: |
      Goal: find the Phone app, swipe to a second page, and come back home. About 5 minutes.

      1. Unlock your phone to reach the Home Screen.
      2. Find the green telephone handset icon (the Phone app). Just find it, do not tap it yet.
      3. Look above the Dock for the tiny dots. Slide your finger left to the next page. Slide left again if there are more apps.
      4. Slide right until you are back on page one.
      5. Swipe up from the bottom edge (or press the Home button) to return home.

      Success: you are back on your main Home Screen and can see the Phone icon and the page dots.
- title: 'Section 4: personalizing your lock screen'
  intro: The Lock Screen is the first thing you see every time you pick up your phone. A photo of your grandchildren, a flower, or a favorite color makes every glance feel a little more personal.
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Change your wallpaper
    body: |
      "Wallpaper" means the background picture or color. Here is how to change it.

      1. Press the Side Button to wake the screen. Stay on the Lock Screen, do not unlock yet.
      2. Press and **hold** your finger on the Lock Screen for 1 to 2 seconds until it zooms back and a "Customize" button and a blue plus (+) appear.
      3. Tap **Customize**.
      4. Tap **Wallpaper**.
      5. Swipe left and right to browse colorful images, nature photos, and more. To use your own photo, tap **Photos** and pick one.
      6. Tap the wallpaper you like, then tap **Add** in the top right.
      7. When it asks, tap **Set as Wallpaper Pair** to use it on both the Lock Screen and Home Screen.

      > Want only the Lock Screen changed? Tap "Customize Home Screen" to set them separately. Try "Photo Shuffle" and your phone will rotate through several photos through the day, like a personal slideshow.

      You can also pick a **color or art style**: in the wallpaper choices, tap "Color," choose a color, and watch the clock style change to match. Then tap Add and Set as Wallpaper Pair.

      > Do not worry if you change it and do not like it. Nothing is permanent. Experiment freely.
  - type: activity
    title: 'Let''s try it together: set a new wallpaper'
    body: |
      Goal: set a new Lock Screen wallpaper. About 5 minutes.

      1. Press the Side Button to wake the screen. Stay on the Lock Screen, do not unlock.
      2. Press and hold the middle of the Lock Screen until the "Customize" button appears.
      3. Tap **Customize**, then **Wallpaper**.
      4. Browse by swiping, pick one you like, and tap it.
      5. Tap **Add**, then **Set as Wallpaper Pair**.
      6. Press the Side Button to lock, then press again to see your new Lock Screen.

      Success: when you wake the phone, you see your chosen picture or color behind the time and date.
- title: 'Section 5: adding a home screen widget'
  intro: Imagine seeing today's weather, tomorrow's appointment, or a slideshow of family photos without tapping anything. That is what widgets do. They bring the information you care about to the surface.
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Add and remove a widget
    body: |
      A widget is a live window on your Home Screen. Here is how to add one.

      1. Unlock your phone and go to the Home Screen.
      2. Press and **hold** an empty area (no icons) for 1 to 2 seconds until the icons start to jiggle. This is editing mode.
      3. Tap the small **+** in the top left corner.
      4. A panel slides up with all the widgets. Tap the search bar and type a name, for example "Weather."
      5. Tap the widget name to see sizes: small, medium, and large. Swipe through the previews.
      6. Tap **Add Widget**. It appears on your Home Screen.
      7. Press and hold to drag it where you want, then tap **Done** in the top right.

      Some great widgets to try first:

      | Widget | What it shows |
      |---|---|
      | **Weather** | Current temperature and forecast. Check it before going outside. |
      | **Calendar** | Your next appointment. Never miss a doctor's visit or birthday. |
      | **Photos** | A rotating slideshow of your favorite photos. |
      | **Clock** | The time for any city, helpful if family lives in another time zone. |
      | **Reminders** | Your upcoming to-do items at a glance. |

      **To remove a widget:** press and hold it, tap "Remove Widget," then "Remove."

      > Removing a widget does not delete the app. The app is still on your phone. You are just taking the tile off your Home Screen.
  - type: activity
    title: 'Let''s try it together: add the Weather widget'
    body: |
      Goal: add the Weather widget. About 5 minutes.

      1. Unlock and go to the Home Screen.
      2. Press and hold an empty spot until the icons jiggle.
      3. Tap the **+** in the top left corner.
      4. Tap the search bar, type "Weather," and tap it in the results.
      5. Swipe to the medium size and tap **Add Widget**.
      6. Tap **Done** in the top right to stop the jiggling.

      Success: you see a weather widget on your Home Screen showing the current temperature.
- title: 'Section 6: organizing your home screen'
  intro: A cluttered screen can feel like a messy desk. A few minutes of organizing means the apps you want are easy to find and the ones you never use are out of the way. You are in charge here.
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Move apps, make folders, and set your Dock
    body: |
      **Move an app icon:** press and hold it until the icons jiggle, then drag it slowly where you want. To move it to another page, drag it to the far left or right edge and hold, and the page flips. Lift your finger to drop it, then tap **Done**.

      > Put your most-used apps in the lower half of the screen, where your thumb reaches most comfortably.

      **Create a folder:** press and hold any app until the icons jiggle, then drag one app on top of another and hold. A folder is created with both apps inside. Tap the folder name to type your own, like "Family Apps." Tap outside to close, then **Done**. To move an app out of a folder, open it in jiggle mode and drag the icon outside.

      **Customize your Dock** (your 4 most-used apps that stay visible): in jiggle mode, drag an app up out of the Dock to remove it, or drag an app down into the Dock to add it. It holds up to 4.

      > Good Dock apps: Phone for calls, Messages for texting, Photos for pictures, and Safari for the internet.

      **Remove an app from the Home Screen:** press and hold it, tap "Remove App," then "Remove from Home Screen."

      > "Remove from Home Screen" just hides the app, and it stays in the App Library. "Delete App" erases it completely. When unsure, always choose "Remove from Home Screen."
- title: 'Section 7: your control center'
  intro: 'Imagine a remote control for your phone: one place to turn on the flashlight, lower the volume, connect to Wi-Fi, or dim the screen, all in one swipe. That is Control Center, and you will use it every day.'
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Open it and see what is inside
    body: |
      **To open Control Center:** on newer iPhones, swipe down from the **top right** corner. On older iPhones, swipe up from the very bottom edge. To close it, swipe it away or tap outside the panel.

      | Control | What it does |
      |---|---|
      | **Flashlight** | Turns the bright light on the back on or off. One tap. |
      | **Wi-Fi** (curved waves) | Turns your Wi-Fi internet on or off. Blue means connected. |
      | **Bluetooth** (pointy B) | Turns wireless connections on or off, used for wireless headphones. |
      | **Airplane Mode** (small airplane) | Turns off all wireless signals. Orange means on. |
      | **Brightness** (sun with a bar) | Drag up for brighter, down for dimmer. |
      | **Volume** (speaker with a bar) | Drag up for louder, down for quieter. |
      | **Do Not Disturb** (crescent moon) | Silences calls and notifications temporarily. Great for bedtime. |
      | **Screen Rotation Lock** (arrow circling a lock) | Stops the screen from rotating sideways when you tilt the phone. |

      > The Flashlight is one of the most popular features in this whole guide. One tap on, one tap off. Press and hold the Brightness or Volume bar for a full-size slider.
  - type: lesson
    title: Flashlight, brightness, airplane mode, and adding controls
    body: |
      **Flashlight:** open Control Center, tap the flashlight icon once to turn the back light on, tap again to turn it off.

      **Brightness:** open Control Center, find the tall bar with a sun at the top, and drag up for brighter or down for dimmer.

      **Airplane Mode** turns off all wireless signals (Wi-Fi, cellular, and Bluetooth), which airlines require. Open Control Center, tap the airplane icon (it turns orange for on), and tap again to turn it off.

      > When Airplane Mode is on, you cannot make calls, send texts, or use the internet. Remember to turn it back off when you land.

      **Add your own controls:** open **Settings**, tap **Control Center**, then use the green plus (+) next to any item under "More Controls" to add it, or the red minus to remove one. Good ones to add: Text Size, Timer, and Notes.
  - type: activity
    title: 'Let''s try it together: flashlight and a new control'
    body: |
      Goal: open Control Center, use the flashlight, and add one control. About 5 minutes.

      1. Wake and unlock your phone.
      2. Swipe down from the top right corner (or up from the bottom on older iPhones).
      3. Tap the flashlight icon. The back light turns on. Tap again to turn it off, then tap outside to close.
      4. Open **Settings**, tap **Control Center**, find "Text Size" under More Controls, and tap the green **+**.
      5. Go back to the Home Screen and open Control Center to see your new control.

      Success: you can open Control Center in one swipe, use the flashlight, and see your new control.
- title: 'Section 8: making the screen easier to see'
  intro: 'Your comfort matters more than anything. The iPhone has wonderful settings to make the screen easier on your eyes: bigger text, bolder letters, and a brighter or dimmer screen. Your phone, your settings.'
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Bold text, brightness, and dark or light mode
    body: |
      > There is no wrong answer for text size. Pick whatever feels easiest for your eyes. You can always change it again.

      **Bold text** makes letters heavier and darker: go to **Settings**, then **Display & Brightness**, find **Bold Text**, and tap the toggle so it turns green. Bold text plus a larger text size is a great combination for easy reading.

      **Screen brightness:** in **Display & Brightness**, drag the slider (small sun on the left, bigger sun on the right) right for brighter, left for dimmer.

      **Dark Mode or Light Mode:**

      | Mode | What it looks like |
      |---|---|
      | **Light Mode** (the default) | White backgrounds with dark text. Easiest in a well-lit room or outdoors. |
      | **Dark Mode** | Very dark backgrounds with light text. Many people find it easier on the eyes at night. |

      To switch, go to **Display & Brightness** and tap the "Light" or "Dark" preview at the top. To have it switch automatically at sunset, turn on the **Automatic** switch below those options.

      > Try Dark Mode at night before bed. Many people find reading in bed much gentler on their eyes with a dark background.
- title: 'Section 9: notifications, your phone''s way of talking to you'
  intro: 'Ever had your phone buzz or ding and wondered what that was? That is a notification, your phone letting you know something happened: a text from your daughter, a reminder, a weather alert. Let''s understand what it is saying.'
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: What notifications are and where to see them
    body: |
      A **notification** is a small alert your phone shows or plays to give you information. Some examples:

      - A text arrives, so the phone buzzes and shows a small banner at the top.
      - A calendar event is coming up, so a reminder appears.
      - Someone is calling, so a large calling screen fills the display.
      - An app has updates, so a small red number appears on its icon.

      **Where to see them:**

      - **On your Lock Screen:** recent notifications are listed there. You can read them without unlocking.
      - **In Notification Center:** unlock the phone and swipe **down from the very top left**. (Top right opens Control Center. Top left opens Notification Center.) Scroll to read, tap one to jump to that app, or swipe left on one and tap "Clear" to dismiss it.

      > Think of Notification Center like checking your mailbox. Look at what came in, read what matters, respond, and clear the rest.
  - type: lesson
    title: Badges and controlling which apps notify you
    body: |
      **App badges** are the small red circles with numbers on app icons. A "5" on Messages means 5 unread texts. A badge on Mail means unread email. A badge on the App Store means updates are available. Tapping the app usually clears the badge once you have seen the items.

      **Control which apps can notify you:** open **Settings**, tap **Notifications**, tap any app (for example, Messages), and use the **Allow Notifications** toggle. Green allows notifications, gray silences them.

      > Keep notifications on for Phone, Messages, and any health or reminder apps. Those are the ones you probably always want to hear from.

  - type: lesson
    title: 'Glossary: words used in this guide'
    body: |
      - **App (application):** a program on your phone that does a specific job, like the Camera app or the Weather app.
      - **App Library:** a screen to the left of all your Home Screen pages that automatically sorts every app into categories.
      - **Airplane Mode:** a setting that turns off all wireless signals. Required on airplanes.
      - **Badge:** a small red circle with a number on an app icon, showing how many unread items are waiting.
      - **Bluetooth:** a short-range wireless connection for wireless headphones, speakers, or hearing aids.
      - **Control Center:** a hidden panel of quick buttons (flashlight, volume, brightness, Wi-Fi). Swipe down from the top right on newer iPhones.
      - **Dark Mode:** a display style with dark backgrounds and light text, easier on the eyes at night.
      - **Do Not Disturb:** a setting that silences calls and notifications temporarily.
      - **Dock:** the row of 4 apps at the very bottom that stays visible on every page.
      - **Face ID:** a feature on newer iPhones that unlocks the phone by scanning your face.
      - **Flashlight:** turns on the bright light on the back of the phone. Find it in Control Center.
      - **Folder:** a group of apps stored together in one icon.
      - **Force Restart:** a way to restart a frozen iPhone with a button combination. Nothing gets deleted.
      - **Home Screen:** the main screen showing all your app icons.
      - **Icon:** a small picture that represents an app. Tap it once to open the app.
      - **Light Mode:** the default display style with white backgrounds and dark text.
      - **Lock Screen:** the screen that shows the time when your phone is locked.
      - **Notification:** a message or alert telling you something happened.
      - **Notification Center:** a list of recent notifications. Swipe down from the top left.
      - **Passcode:** a 4 or 6 digit code that unlocks your phone, like an ATM PIN.
      - **Screenshot:** a photo of exactly what is on your screen. Press Side Button and Volume Up together.
      - **Side Button:** the tall button on the right edge, used to lock, wake, power off, or screenshot.
      - **Silent Mode:** when the phone will not play sounds for calls or notifications. It may still vibrate.
      - **Swipe:** a smooth sliding motion with one finger, like wiping a table.
      - **Tap:** gently touch the screen once and lift up, like pressing a button quickly.
      - **Toggle Switch:** a small on/off switch in Settings. Green is on, gray is off.
      - **Touch ID:** a feature on older iPhones that unlocks with your fingerprint on the round Home button.
      - **Widget:** a live tile on your Home Screen that shows updated information without opening the app.
      - **Wi-Fi:** a wireless internet connection, at home or in coffee shops and other places.
