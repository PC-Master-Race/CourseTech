- title: The four-question fit check
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Ask these before you start
    body: |
      You know what AI is and how to talk to it. The next question is the one that actually changes your workday: where does it belong in your work?

      Not every task is a good fit, and the people who get real value are the ones with a sharp eye for which is which. When a task lands on your plate and you wonder "could AI help here?", run it through four questions.

      | Question | A "yes" points toward AI |
      |---|---|
      | Is it repetitive or routine? | You do it often and the steps rarely change |
      | Is it mostly about language? | Writing, summarizing, rewording, explaining |
      | Is it information-heavy? | Lots of reading or notes to pull together |
      | Is it low-stakes if imperfect? | A small mistake is easy to catch and fix |

      You do not need a "yes" on all four. The more yes answers, the stronger the fit.
  - type: lesson
    title: A rule of thumb
    body: |
      If a task is repetitive, language-based, information-heavy, and low-stakes when it is not perfect, it is almost always worth trying with AI.

      If a task is one of a kind, needs physical work, calls for deep ethical judgment, or has zero room for error, keep yourself in the lead.
- title: Remove, accelerate, or improve
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Know the kind of value you want
    body: |
      Once you spot a task that might fit, ask one more thing before you start prompting. Which of these are you really after?

      - **Remove.** Take a tedious task off your plate completely.
      - **Accelerate.** Get the same result, much faster.
      - **Improve.** End up with something better than you would have made alone.

      Many good use cases do all three at once. But naming the main goal helps you write a sharper prompt and judge the result more honestly.
  - type: activity
    title: Name three tasks
    body: |
      Write down three real tasks from your own job. For each one:

      1. Run the four-question fit check. How many yes answers?
      2. Decide: are you trying to **remove**, **accelerate**, or **improve** it?

      Bring your three to our next session. We will compare notes across different fields.
- title: Where AI falls short
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Keep the human in the lead here
    body: |
      Knowing where AI shines is only half the skill. Knowing where it struggles keeps you out of trouble.

      - **Final judgment calls.** Hiring, discipline, medical or legal decisions. AI can help you prepare, not decide.
      - **Anything needing real-world, up-to-the-minute facts** the model was never given.
      - **Truly original or sensitive work** where being wrong carries a real cost.

      We go deeper on the risks in "When AI Gets It Wrong." For now, just remember: AI is a strong assistant, and you are still the professional in the room.
