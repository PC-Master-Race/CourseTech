When AI Gets It Wrong

The warning label. How AI makes mistakes, and the one habit that protects you.

A video walkthrough is coming for this lesson.You can work through everything below right now. No video needed.

Part 1

The one habit to build

Lesson

AI proposes, you verify, you decide

Every powerful tool comes with safety instructions. A forklift, a scalpel, a prescription. All useful, all able to cause harm when misused. AI is no different.

This is the warning label. I am not trying to scare you off, you have already seen how helpful these tools are. But the most dangerous user is not the skeptic. It is the person who trusts AI completely, skips the check, and lets a wrong answer cause a real problem.

By the end of this topic, I want one habit locked in:

AI proposes. You verify. You decide.

That habit protects you, your employer, and the people you serve.

Part 2

Hallucination: when AI makes things up

Lesson

Why it happens

The single most important limit to understand is what researchers call a hallucination. That is when the AI states something false, but says it with the same smooth confidence it uses when it is right.

Remember how a language model works: it predicts the next likely word. It does not pull facts from a filing cabinet. So when you ask for a specific date, a quote, or a statistic, it generates what sounds like the right kind of answer.

  • If the true answer was common in its training, it is often right.
  • If the answer was rare, recent, or poorly documented, it may invent something that looks perfect and is completely made up.

Here is the danger: there is no built-in signal that tells you it is guessing. A fake source looks exactly like a real one. You cannot tell from the text alone. You have to check.

Lesson

A real case that made the news

Two lawyers once filed a court brief written with an AI tool. It cited several past cases to support their argument. The cases sounded real. They had names, numbers, and quotes.

None of them existed. The AI had invented them. A judge caught it, and the lawyers were fined and publicly embarrassed.

They were not careless people. They just trusted the output and skipped the check. That is the whole lesson in one story.

Part 3

Your verification checklist

Lesson

Match your effort to the stakes

Not every answer needs the same scrutiny. Use the stakes to decide how hard to check.

If the output is... Do this
Low stakes (a rough draft for yourself) A quick read-through is fine
Medium stakes (a team email) Verify any names, numbers, and claims
High stakes (a client, patient, or legal document) Check every specific fact against a trusted source before it leaves your hands

Before anything leaves your hands, take 60 seconds and ask: are there any specific claims here I have not verified? That minute is your professional protection.

Activity · try it

Fact-check a passage

I'll give you a short, professional-looking passage that an AI wrote. Your job:

  1. Read it carefully. It will look authoritative.
  2. Spend 10 to 15 minutes checking the specific claims with a search engine.
  3. Underline anything you cannot confirm or find to be wrong.
  4. Note what you found, and be ready to share.

Two questions to hold onto: How easy was it to spot the errors just by reading? What would have happened if this had gone to your supervisor unchecked?
