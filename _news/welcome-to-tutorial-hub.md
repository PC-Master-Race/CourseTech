Nobody should need a decoder ring to learn how technology works. That is the idea this whole site is built on.

## Why I built it

Good, patient explanations have become surprisingly hard to find. Most of what is out there is written as if you already know the vocabulary. The people who most need a clear explanation are often the ones getting the least help.

I teach these subjects for a living, to adults who are starting new careers or getting comfortable with technology for the first time. I already know where people get stuck, because I watch it happen and then rewrite the explanation until it lands. It seemed worth putting that somewhere anyone could reach it, not just the people sitting in my classroom.

Here is the thing that bothers me most: how much knowledge is locked up behind vocabulary. Not because the ideas are hard, but because someone reached for the insider word instead of the plain one, or skipped the step everybody supposedly knows. The information is public and still out of reach, and the person reading it walks away thinking they are not smart enough. They almost always were. The explanation was the problem.

So I say things in plain language here, every time I can. If a technical term is worth knowing, I define it the first time and then use it, because you deserve the real word too. But I will not dress up a simple idea to make it sound impressive.

## What is here so far

The library starts with the subjects I teach most:

- **AI at Work**, a full course on using AI tools well and knowing when not to, from what AI actually is through prompting, ethics, agents, and the tools already showing up in your field.
- **Office Productivity**, starting with Excel from the very beginning.
- **Devices & Digital Life**, starting with getting comfortable with your phone.

More is coming. I teach around seventeen subjects, and I am working through them one at a time.

## What this page is for

I will use this space to say when a new lesson goes up, share a tip worth trying, or point you to something useful. Short notes, only when there is something worth telling you.

Poke around, start anywhere, and come back whenever you like. And if something here helps you, pass it along to someone else who could use it.
