- title: What an ATS is, and why it matters
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: The software that reads your resume first
    body: |
      Before your resume reaches a human, it usually goes through a piece of software first. That software is called an **Applicant Tracking System**, or ATS. It scans your resume for specific keywords from the job description. If your resume does not have enough of those words, it gets filtered out automatically, before a single person reads it.

      | The reality | What it means for you |
      |---|---|
      | Most large employers and staffing agencies use ATS software to pre-screen every application | Even a strong resume can get rejected before a human sees it, simply because it is missing the right words from the posting |
      | ATS software matches keywords in your resume against keywords in the job description | Your resume needs to speak the same language as the posting, and AI can help you find exactly which words to add |
  - type: lesson
    title: This is a perfect job for AI
    body: |
      Resume optimization passes all four questions from the task filter, which is why it is such a clean fit for AI:

      - **Repetitive?** Yes. You apply to several jobs, and each one needs the same optimization.
      - **Language-based?** Yes. It is entirely about matching written words from one document to another.
      - **Recoverable if wrong?** Yes. You read and approve every change before you submit anything.
      - **Clear good-enough threshold?** Yes. A resume that includes the top 5 to 8 keywords from the posting is measurably better than one that does not.

      The bottom line: you stay in control. AI just does the keyword matching and rewriting.
- title: The three-step process
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: Find the keywords, match them, then make it yours
    body: |
      | Step | What you do | The prompt to use |
      |---|---|---|
      | **1** | Find a job posting you want and paste the full description into ChatGPT | "Read this job description and give me the top 8 to 10 keywords and phrases an ATS would be scanning for. List them in order of importance. [Paste the full job description here]" |
      | **2** | Take the keyword list and compare it to your resume summary or skills section | "Here is my current resume summary: [paste it]. Here are the top keywords from the job description: [paste the list]. Rewrite my summary to naturally include as many of these as possible without sounding forced. Keep it under 4 sentences." |
      | **3** | Review the rewrite. Edit anything that does not sound like you, and check that every claim is true | No prompt needed. This step is your job. Read it out loud. If it does not sound like you, change it. Never submit something you cannot stand behind. |

      Even just running Step 1 is useful. Seeing the keyword list tells you what a posting really cares about.
- title: No resume yet? Build one section at a time
  blocks:
  - type: lesson
    title: One section per sitting
    body: |
      Not having a resume yet is not a problem. Use these prompts to build it one section at a time. Do not try to write the whole thing at once. One section per sitting works far better.

      | Section | When to do it | Prompt to use |
      |---|---|---|
      | **Summary statement** | First, it sets the tone | "I am a [job title] with [X years] of experience in [field]. Write a 3-sentence professional summary for my resume that highlights my strengths and sounds confident but not arrogant." |
      | **Work experience** | One job at a time | "I worked as a [job title] at [company] from [year] to [year]. My main responsibilities were [list 3 to 4]. Rewrite these as strong resume bullet points using action verbs and specific results where possible." |
      | **Skills section** | After the summary and experience | "Based on this job description [paste it], what skills should I list that match what they are looking for? Only list skills I would actually have as a [your job title]." |
      | **Cover letter (bonus)** | Optional, but effective when tailored | "Write a 3-paragraph cover letter for this job [paste posting]. I am a [job title] with experience in [your field]. Keep it professional, specific to the role, and under 300 words." |
  - type: lesson
    title: One important reminder
    body: |
      Here is the rule that matters most: **AI writes a draft. You make it true.**

      Never submit a resume that says something you cannot back up in an interview. AI does not know your actual work history. It fills in based on what sounds good. So read every line. If a claim is not accurate, cut it or fix it before you send anything.

      And keep the real goal in mind. This is not about tricking the software. It is about making sure the right words are present so that a real human gets the chance to read what you actually have to offer.
- title: Try it
  blocks:
  - type: activity
    title: Run step one right now
    body: |
      If your resume is on your phone, in Google Drive, or in your email, pull it up. If you have a few minutes, run Step 1 on a real job posting.

      1. Find a job title you are interested in and a real posting for it.
      2. Paste the full description into ChatGPT with the Step 1 prompt above.
      3. Look at the top 5 keywords it gives you. How many are already in your resume: most of them, some, only a few, or none?
      4. Jot down your first reaction. Were you surprised by any of the words it picked out?

      You do not have to finish all three steps in one go. Even the keyword list is worth having.
