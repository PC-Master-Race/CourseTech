<h1>About this site</h1>
<p class="lead">Why it exists, who it is for, and how to get the most out of it.</p>

**Nobody should need a decoder ring to learn how technology works.** That is the whole
idea behind this site. It is a growing library of technology lessons, open to anyone in
the world, written in language a normal person can actually follow.

Good, patient explanations have become surprisingly hard to find. Most of what is out
there is written for people who already know the vocabulary. Meanwhile the people who
most need a clear explanation, someone starting a new career, someone who never got
comfortable with computers, someone handed a smartphone and left to figure it out, are
the ones getting the least help.

This is my attempt to fix that in the corner of the world I can reach.

## Jargon is a gate, and I keep taking it down

Here is the thing that bothers me most, and the reason I write the way I do.

Too much knowledge is locked up behind vocabulary. Not because the ideas are genuinely
hard, but because of how they are written. Somebody uses the insider word instead of the
plain one, skips the step that "everybody knows," or explains a simple thing in a way
that only makes sense if you already understand it. The information is technically
public, and it is still out of reach.

That is gatekeeping, whether anyone means it that way or not. And it teaches people the
worst possible lesson: that they are not smart enough for this. Almost always, they were
never the problem. The explanation was.

So I do whatever I can to say things in plain language. If a technical term is genuinely
useful, I define it the first time it appears and then use it, because you deserve to
know the real word too. But I will not hide a simple idea behind a complicated sentence
to make it sound impressive. If you have ever read something twice and felt stupid, I
want this site to be the opposite of that experience.

## Who writes this

I teach technology for a living. My students are adults, many of them starting over in a
new field, and many of them arriving convinced they are "bad with computers." Everything
here comes out of teaching those subjects, roughly seventeen of them, in real classrooms
with real people who ask real questions.

That is the advantage of this library. It is not written by a committee or generated in
bulk. It is written by someone who has watched where people actually get stuck, and
rewritten when an explanation did not land.

## Who it is for

Anyone. You do not need to be enrolled anywhere, and you do not need any background.

These lessons are written for adults getting comfortable with technology, including
people who feel like they are starting from scratch. If you have ever thought "everyone
else seems to get this but me," you are exactly who I had in mind. There are no silly
questions here, and nothing moves faster than you do.

## How the lessons work

Each course is a set of short lessons. Every lesson opens by telling you what you will
learn and why it matters, then breaks into short parts made of three kinds of blocks:

- A **Lesson** block is the main teaching. Read it at your own pace.
- An **Activity** block is a small hands-on task. This is where the learning sticks.
- A **Check your understanding** block links to a short quiz.

Each one is marked with a color, a label, and an icon, so you always know what you are
looking at. Every lesson can also be printed, so you can work from paper if you prefer.

## What is here, and what is coming

The library is growing. It starts with the subjects I teach most: using AI at work,
Excel and office software, and getting comfortable with everyday devices. More is on the
way, and the [News page]({{ '/news/' | relative_url }}) is where I post what has been added.

**A few promises.** I write in plain language, and I define any technical term the first
time it appears. I would rather show you a clear example than bury you in theory. I will
never use a complicated word where a simple one does the job. And if a lesson is ever
unclear, that is the lesson's fault, not yours. Tell me, and I will fix it.
